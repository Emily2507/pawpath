Brand Name

CANOVA is derived from ‘Canine’ and ‘Nova’, which is Latin for new. We chose this name because our brand focus is oriented around pet ownership, this specific product focusing on dogs specifically. Within our name, we wanted to leave opportunity for expansion of our brand while at the same time paying homage to our very first product. ‘Nova’ was also specifically chosen because we wanted to present something new to the market—we have faith in the originality of our product idea and want to be known for our innovative qualities. We also want to propose a new take on pet ownership by making it more of a community-based commonality between people and make every walk and outing feel ‘new’. The basis of our entire brand and product lies within wanting to take the boringness and the repetitiveness out of something that is ordinarily quite routinely.

Visual identity

Our logo is a very minimalistic linework outlining the side profile of a dog, created with the help of ChatGPT. We chose this because the focus of our brand is on dogs and their owners. The clear and round lines fit very well with the feel and vibe we want for our brand and also go well with our chosen font.

For Typography we chose the Milk Tea Font TH. It’s also very round and playful without any harsh or strict lines, capturing the casual and fun tone of Canova, while still being professional and easy to read.

For the colour palette we chose five colours. The main colours are two dark greens (#37977f and #324b4b ). One of them is also used for the logo and the even darker one provides a nice contrast to the other lighter colours. As additional and supporting colours we chose a bright blue (#9ad1ee ), a bright yellow (#f9f871 ) and an orange (#de8040 ). The green tones are representative of the nature and of outdoorsiness, which is an important value of our brand. They capture the main visual feel of Canova not only as background colours but also as main accents. The other brighter colours contrast with the greens and are supposed to break the calming and soothing atmosphere. They are fun, playful and inviting, once again stressing the informal and cheerful nature of Canova.



Copywriting

The tone of our brand is conversational, casual. We want to give it to you straight and keep your attention captured while doing it. But it is also fun—especially our social media captions make use of emojis and the same rhetorical questions that are also seen in our brand values. The only exception to all of this is our brand vision, which is written to be a little more dream-like. In this case, it is because we want to encourage our target audience to dream big and to imagine the possibilities that Canova can create for them.

UI PATTERNS

The website uses a common structure that follows a clear top-down hierarchy, engaging images, bold headings and short related text to help users easily digest key information. Using real pet images are crucial to create emotional bonds and make the experience more relevant. The content is organized into scannable, small sections using icons and shaped images. While the headlines combined with images provide intuitive navigation and helps guide users through the platform's main features.

Testimonial: Consumers need to trust the brand, and testimonials help build trust. They act as social proof, making value claims feel more real.

Navigation Tab: Navigation tabs make it super easy for users to know where they are and what’s on the site. Highlighting the current page helps people stay on track and find what they’re looking for without getting lost.

Carousel (Used for Testimonials) : The carousel shows off different stories all in one spot. It’s a quick way for users to scroll through testimonials without feeling overwhelmed. Plus, the movement catches the eye and keeps people engaged with simple controls.

Footer: Sometimes it’s hard to get to the page or info you want because of how the site is set up. A footer with shortcuts helps solve that, fewer clicks, less confusion, and faster access.

Picture Break: Since the aim of website is to be less intimidating, providing a picture break will create a depth that represents the brand to the viewers and to catch a break from reading.

Testing Report

The goal of this testing session was to check how well the PawPath website works not just technically, but from a real dog owner’s perspective. We wanted to know: Does the site clearly show what PawPath and Canova is all about? Is it easy to use? And are we successfully sharing the heart of our brand, Canova?

We invited 4 dog owners to explore the site and give us their honest feedback. The testing took place on laptops, both on campus and at home, so we could see how PawPath feels in real-life settings. Each participant got a quick intro and then tried a few tasks while saying their thoughts out loud. We observed and took notes on anything that felt smooth, confusing, or surprising. Tasks included:

Finding the About Us page

Locating social media links

Exploring our brand values

Checking how many users are currently on PawPath

Positive Aspects

Easy Sign-Up & Log-In: Everyone found the process simple and straightforward Smooth Navigation: Testers loved how easy it was to move around the site, especially with helpful images and bold headings guiding the way. Consistent Branding: The paw prints and friendly design really stood out. Testers mentioned it felt true to the PawPath. Clickable Buttons: Buttons were easy to spot, use, and matched the look and feel of the rest of the site. Pleasant Visuals: The color scheme and overall design gave an inviting feeling. Social & Contact Info: Easy to find and well-placed, testers didn’t have to dig to get connected.

Negative Aspects

Long Homepage: A few testers felt that the homepage had a bit too much going on, it was a lot to scroll through and read at once.

What We've Improved (or Plan To)

Shortened Homepage: The homepage now gets to the point quicker, with clear sections, less text, and bold calls-to-action to guide users where they want to go.

Bug Fixes: Any non-working features, like the Sign-Up page, were fixed to make sure everything runs smoothly

Future Planning

Over the course of the extended campaign, this is what we learned and what we should implement in the future:

1. Prioritize Reels as the Primary Content Format: Throughout the campaign, Reels consistently outperformed static posts in terms of views and likes especially when the content featured dogs. This aligns with Gen Z's preference for short-form, visual-first content. In the future Reels should become the main format allowing PawPath to stay aligned with user habits while maximizing engagement.

2. Dog-Focused Videos Drive the Highest Engagement: Reels that featured dogs whether they were being pet, running, or playing with friends consistently delivered the highest engagement levels during the campaign. The combination of relatability, emotional appeal, and cuteness engaged deeply with our Gen Z audience. Videos without dogs, even when informative or humorous, has an average of lower reach and interaction. Moving forward, integrating dogs into the majority of our Reels is essential to maintain strong audience interest and create a deeper emotional connection with the brand.

3. Continue Targeting Gen Z Dog Owners in Breda and Tilburg: Our most active and responsive audience segment was 18–24-year-old dog owners located in urban areas like Breda and Tilburg. Future campaigns can strengthen the connection by featuring recognizable locations, and promoting more local partnerships.

Validation of Assumptions

We decided to focus our website on offering services for discovering new dog walking routes that meet the niche needs of a pet owner. On top of that, we made it a community-centric app to put people in touch with each other. The reason for this is because our problem interviews led us the following four insights of our target audience: first, dog owners find walking the same routes repetitive and are very much open to exploring new pasts, though they lack the resources to find them. This directly relates to their second one, which is that dog owners feel limited in their route choices. For urban livers, the primary reason for this was that their city was too sizable and they often found themselves overwhelmed with trying to find new options and weren’t quite sure where to begin the search. This was especially the case with students or young adults who have a job, because it was already sometimes difficult for them to squeeze in walks for their pets, much less be adventurous about it. Third, we found that dog owners value social connections but often struggle to maintain them past the point of saying hello to other dog walkers they run into on their walks. However, one of their biggest wishes is to build deeper and more repetitive relationships. In our solution interview, our interviewees appreciated the features that they suggest routes based on mood and preferences. This especially in relation to being able to see the business and duration of trails, and having chat and event planning functions.

In our solution interviews, we inquired whether people would be willing to pay for the app, however we learned that most people would not buy the app for an initial price. However, they were open to getting add-on features within the app (such as unlocking extra stats or kilometer tracking features). Then, they would be willing to consider a family or friend subscription to the app in order to split the costs and have people who they know on the app as well. This is why the final version of the lean canvas we have the description of free basic features and the initial download. However, we’ve also listed add-ons.

A moment that we pivoted was when we decided to focus specifically on urban livers. Initially, we had wanted to focus on small-town people as well, but our problem interviews revealed that they’re already quite familiar wih the routes, mostly because they grew up there or have already done the limited amount of exploration that is possible. This is why we then refocused onto a ‘travel mode’ feature, as a result of them sharing that they often struggle finding routes to take their dog while on holiday in an unfamiliar town.
